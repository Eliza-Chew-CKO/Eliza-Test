Successful CE 3.0-tagged cases have converted into wins at a materially higher rate than the broad non-CE baseline in this dataset. That is the clearest reason to keep exploring the programme.

This should be framed as an initial directional signal, not proof. The dataset is small, recent cohorts are immature, and CE 3.0 only touches a very small slice of total disputes.

How we approached it

Given the data constraints, we used a deliberately simple lens: look for directional evidence rather than statistical proof.

Step 1
Separate CE 3.0-tagged cases from the baseline

We treated successful and fallback as the CE 3.0-touched population and compared them against the larger non-labelled baseline.

Step 2
Look at representment and win outcomes

We focused on whether successful CE 3.0 cases appear to convert to representation and wins at stronger rates than the baseline.

Step 3
Sanity-check mature cohorts separately

Because recent dispute weeks have not fully matured, we also isolated older cohorts to see whether the positive signal persists once outcomes have had time to resolve.

Key takeaways

A meeting-ready interpretation that stays positive without overselling the data.

Encouraging directional signal

Successful CE 3.0-tagged cases are showing stronger directional outcomes than the non-CE baseline in this extract, especially on older cohorts.

Quality looks better than quantity

The evidence here suggests CE 3.0 may be helping on the specific cases it touches, but it is not yet touching enough volume to move the overall portfolio visibly.

Main limitation

Coverage is below 1% of disputes in this file, so the current read is best interpreted as promising early signs rather than programme impact proven.

Why we still need caution

These are the limits of the current read, and they matter.

Very small CE 3.0 volume

Only 43 disputes in the file are tagged as CE 3.0-related, versus 5,907 in the baseline population.

Recent weeks are naturally incomplete

Later dispute cohorts still show representation activity, but not enough elapsed time for win outcomes to settle. That suppresses recent win rates across the board.

No clean eligibility split

Because the file does not show which cases were eligible but not routed through CE 3.0, we cannot cleanly separate rollout coverage from efficacy.

Suggested message

Based on the limited weekly extract we have today, our initial view is that Visa Compelling Evidence 3.0 is showing encouraging early signs on the cases it touches. The most positive signal is that successful CE 3.0-tagged disputes appear to be converting into wins at a meaningfully stronger rate than the wider baseline. The important caveat is that current CE 3.0 coverage is still very small, so this looks more like a promising proof of concept than a portfolio-level impact story yet.
